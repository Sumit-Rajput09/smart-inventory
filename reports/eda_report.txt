INSIGHTS AND BUSINESS INTERPRETATION
==============================================================================
1. TREND: no significant linear trend in total daily sales (-0.3% per year, p = 0.551). Demand is flat over the period, so growth-based planning is not supported by this data.
2. SEASONALITY: monthly means differ by 4.0% of the overall mean (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.060); weekday means differ by 1.6% (p = 0.665). The differences are small in practical terms, so month/weekday alone will not give strong forecasting signal.
3. PRODUCTS: highest mean is P0016 (139.1 units/store-day), lowest is P0002 (133.5); the gap is 4.1% of the overall mean. ANOVA across products: p = 0.601, so the ranking could be due to chance and should not drive decisions.
4. STORES: ANOVA across 5 groups p = 0.047, eta2 = 0.00013 -> borderline (p between 0.01 and 0.05); with several tests run this may be chance, and the effect size is negligible.
5. CATEGORIES: ANOVA across 5 groups p = 0.253, eta2 = 0.00007 -> no evidence that average demand differs between them.
6. REGIONS: ANOVA across 4 groups p = 0.582, eta2 = 0.00003 -> no evidence that average demand differs between them.
7. PROMOTION/HOLIDAY: mean units sold 136.4 with the flag vs 136.5 without (difference -0.08, 95% CI [-1.66, 1.50], Welch p = 0.919, Mann-Whitney p = 0.769, Cohen's d = -0.001, negligible effect). There is no evidence that promotions/holidays change sales here - unusual for real retail data.
8. DISCOUNT: ANOVA across 5 discount levels p = 0.918, eta2 = 0.00001. Higher discounts are not associated with higher sales in this data.
9. CORRELATIONS WITH UNITS SOLD: moderate/strong: demand_forecast (r = +0.98), inventory_level (r = +0.59).
10. LEAKAGE WARNING: demand_forecast is almost the same series as units_sold, so it is excluded from model inputs.
11. INVENTORY: median sell-through is 50% of stock per day; units sold exceeded inventory level in 0 records. Stock level behaves like a ceiling on sales in this dataset, which is why inventory and sales correlate - this reflects how the data was generated, not that stocking more causes more demand.
12. SELL-THROUGH SHAPE: units_sold / inventory_level has mean 0.498 and a maximum of 1.00; a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against Uniform(0,1) gives D = 0.0057, p = 0.017. The ratio is almost perfectly evenly spread between 0 and 1 (D is tiny; a small p-value here only reflects the huge sample and integer rounding): each day's sales look like a random share of that day's stock. Real retail demand is not generated this way, which is strong evidence the file is simulated; it also means a day's sales cannot be predicted from history, only from same-day stock, which is unknown at forecast time.
13. OUTLIERS: 1.11% of records lie outside 1.5 x IQR of their own store-product series; they are flagged but kept, because high-sales days are valid demand that the inventory plan must cover.
14. MISSING VALUES: 1 missing cell(s) remain after cleaning (see the missing-values table; they are left for train-only imputation inside the model pipeline).
15. CAUTION: the dataset is synthetic. These findings describe this file only and are not evidence about real retail behaviour or savings. Several tests are run above; at alpha = 0.05 about 1 in 20 true-null tests will look significant by chance.
